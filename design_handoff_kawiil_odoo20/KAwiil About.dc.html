Our story

From a Mission District garage to Thunderbird Road.

Itzel grew up on the back of her father's motorcycle, and on her grandmother's stories from Yucatán about K'awiil, the Maya god of lightning. Years later, working as a battery engineer, she kept coming back to the same thought: the lightning in those stories was now the thing that could make motorcycles cleaner, quieter and faster.

In 2018 she started building a prototype in a rented garage in San Francisco's Mission District with Tomás Herrera, a race mechanic who had spent ten years tuning petrol superbikes and was ready for something new. Their first bike was rough, heavy and had a range of 40 miles. It was also quicker off the line than anything Tomás had ridden.

The name followed the idea. K'awiil gave the company its name, and every model since has been named after a lightning or thunder god from another part of the world: Raijin from Japan, Ukko from Finland, Indra from India. Different cultures, one force.

The biggest problem turned out not to be the bikes. Riders loved the test rides, then banks turned them down because they didn't know how to value an electric motorcycle. So in 2025 K'awiil started financing its own bikes. Arjun Mehta built the program so that riders apply online, get a decision quickly and ride away with a payment they planned for.

Today K'awiil designs, assembles and services every bike at its workshop on Thunderbird Road in the Dogpatch. Every model is built for one purpose: the ride should be the best part of your day.
